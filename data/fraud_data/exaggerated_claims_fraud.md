# Fraud Case Studies - Exaggerated Claims

## Case 401: Opportunistic Vandalism (Pre-existing Damage)
* **Fraud Type**: Claiming Pre-existing Damage
* **Scenario**: Claimant's car was legitimately caught in a hail storm. Alongside the hail dents on the roof and hood, they claimed a massive crack in the front bumper was also caused by the hail.
* **Detection Indicators**:
  - Rust and dirt accumulation inside the bumper crack indicated the damage was several months old.
  - Hail damage physics do not cause lateral cracking on vertical bumper surfaces.
* **Resolution**: Hail damage approved. Bumper repair denied due to pre-existing conditions.

## Case 402: The "Stolen" Upgrades
* **Fraud Type**: Phantom Vehicle Contents
* **Scenario**: Following a vehicle theft and subsequent recovery of the stripped chassis, the claimant provided a list of stolen aftermarket parts: a $3,000 custom stereo, $2,000 alloy wheels, and $1,500 racing seats.
* **Detection Indicators**:
  - The claimant could not provide original purchase receipts.
  - Google Street View images of the car parked at the claimant's house from one month prior showed standard factory wheels and factory seats.
* **Resolution**: Claim approved only for the ACV of the factory-standard vehicle. Upgrades denied due to lack of proof and contradictory evidence.

## Case 403: The "Totaled" Flood Car
* **Fraud Type**: Intentional Destruction
* **Scenario**: Claimant drives an older, mechanically failing vehicle into a shallow flooded street, deliberately opening the doors to flood the interior and total it out.
* **Detection Indicators**:
  - The engine's computer (ECU) logged that the engine was intentionally shut off *before* water intake occurred, not stalled due to hydrolock.
  - No other vehicles on the street were flooded, as the water level was only 6 inches deep—far below the door sill line unless manually opened.
* **Resolution**: Claim denied for intentional damage.

## Case 404: The Missing Airbags
* **Fraud Type**: Part Harvesting
* **Scenario**: Claimant removes intact OEM airbags to sell on the black market after a minor front-end collision, claiming they deployed and replacing them with cheap dummies for the adjuster inspection.
* **Detection Indicators**:
  - The vehicle's Crash Data Retrieval (CDR) system (black box) explicitly logged that the airbags *did not* deploy because the impact delta-V was too low.
  - The steering wheel cover was cut with a blade, not blown open by an explosive charge.
* **Resolution**: Claim denied. Referred to law enforcement for part harvesting fraud.

## Case 405: Arson for Profit
* **Fraud Type**: Intentional Arson
* **Scenario**: A vehicle is found completely burned out in a deserted area. The owner claims it spontaneously caught fire while driving and they had to flee.
* **Detection Indicators**:
  - Fire investigator detects an irregular burn pattern originating from the rear passenger seat, not the engine bay or fuel tank.
  - Chemical sniffer dogs and lab analysis detect traces of gasoline (accelerant) poured directly onto the upholstery.
* **Resolution**: Claim denied. Owner arrested for arson and insurance fraud.
