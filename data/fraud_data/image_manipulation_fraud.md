# Fraud Case Studies - Image Manipulation

## Case 101: The Deepfake Pothole Damage
* **Fraud Type**: Deepfake Image Generation
* **Scenario**: Claimant submitted photos of severe undercarriage and bumper damage, claiming they hit an unmarked pothole on a rural road.
* **Detection Indicators**:
  - Metadata (EXIF) was stripped from the images.
  - Lighting and shadows on the damaged bumper were inconsistent with the ambient lighting of the rural background.
  - Deepfake detection model identified AI-generated artifacts (blurring patterns) near the edges of the damaged area.
* **Resolution**: Claim rejected. Policy canceled due to intentional fraud.

## Case 102: The Reused "Scrap Yard" Photo
* **Fraud Type**: Image Recycling / Sourced from Internet
* **Scenario**: Claimant submitted images of a totaled 2021 Honda Civic after an alleged hit-and-run. 
* **Detection Indicators**:
  - Reverse image search found the exact image on a salvage yard's public auction site posted 6 months prior.
  - The license plate in the image was digitally altered (poorly cloned pixels) to match the claimant's RC.
* **Resolution**: Claim rejected. Flagged to the National Insurance Crime Bureau (NICB).

## Case 103: The Photoshopped Timestamp
* **Fraud Type**: EXIF / Timestamp Alteration
* **Scenario**: The policyholder bought coverage at 2:00 PM and submitted a claim at 4:30 PM for a major collision.
* **Detection Indicators**:
  - The visual timestamp on the dashcam footage showed 1:15 PM (before the policy was active).
  - The claimant used basic image editing software to blur the dashcam timestamp and alter the file creation date.
  - Shadows in the photo indicated the sun angle was consistent with early afternoon (approx 1:00 PM), not 4:30 PM.
* **Resolution**: Claim rejected for pre-existing damage prior to policy inception.

## Case 104: The Reflection Slip-Up
* **Fraud Type**: Contextual Image Manipulation
* **Scenario**: Claimant states a hit-and-run occurred while parked outside a grocery store on a sunny day. 
* **Detection Indicators**:
  - The reflection in the vehicle's undamaged window clearly shows the interior of a dimly lit residential garage with a workbench.
  - Weather records showed heavy rain on the day of the alleged incident, yet the car and ground in the photo are bone dry.
* **Resolution**: Claim rejected due to falsified incident location and timeline.

## Case 105: The "Paper Car" Model
* **Fraud Type**: Forced Perspective / Scale Model
* **Scenario**: Claimant submitted low-resolution photos of a completely crushed Ferrari, claiming it rolled over a cliff. 
* **Detection Indicators**:
  - Close analysis of the background revealed giant blades of grass, indicating forced perspective.
  - The vehicle in the photo was a highly detailed 1:18 scale diecast model intentionally smashed with a hammer. 
* **Resolution**: Claim rejected. Referred for criminal prosecution.
